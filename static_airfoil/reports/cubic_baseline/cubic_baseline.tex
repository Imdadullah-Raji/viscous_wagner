\section*{Summary}

A step response is measured against the steady state it settles to, so the first task of the
programme is the steady lift curve at small angles. Steady (SIMPLE) 2D laminar solutions were
computed, with the angle set by rotating the freestream on one mesh per body and Reynolds number.
The four bodies are NACA0012, NACA0006, a $2.3\%$-thick stadium plate, and a zero-thickness plate.
The method fits odd Legendre polynomials on windows $0$--$A$ and converts them to
$\CL = a_1\theta + a_3\theta^3 + a_5\theta^5 + \dots$

\begin{keybox}{Findings}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{The lift is analytic at the origin with a finite, nonzero cubic term, for all four
  bodies.} The deficit $d = a_1\theta - \CL$ goes as $\theta^3$: its local exponent is $3.01$--$3.10$ over
  $1$--$4^\circ$ and tends to $3$. There is no non-analytic term.
\item \textbf{$a_3$ is robust:} fixed to $0.1\%$ by the fits, independent of window and fit order. For
  the zero-thickness plate it is fixed to about $1\%$ including mesh error.\\
  At Re $=500$: $a_3=-28.2$ (NACA0012), $-24.9$ (NACA0006), $-18.9$ (plate, $t/c=0.023$) and $-12.8$
  (zero-thickness plate). $|a_3|$ grows steadily with thickness and $a_1$ falls ($3.27$, $4.24$, $4.82$,
  $4.94$).
\item \textbf{$a_3$ against Re.}
  \begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item The two airfoils: $|a_3|$ grows with Re.
  \item The thick plate: $|a_3|$ peaks near Re $400$--$450$, then falls.
  \item The zero-thickness plate: $|a_3|$ is flat at about $14.6$ for Re $200$--$300$, then falls almost linearly to
    $8.3$ by Re $900$. Its maximum is at Re $\le 300$, below the thick plate's.
  \end{itemize}
  $a_1$ rises with Re for all but NACA0012.
\item \textbf{What ``cubic at the origin'' means.} By itself it is not a claim of cubic dominance:
  every analytic odd curve looks cubic close enough to the origin. The substantive statement is the
  \emph{range} of the truncation.
  \begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item A cubic fitted on $0$--$4^\circ$ stays within $10^{-3}$ of the data to $5$--$5.5^\circ$ for the three
    thin bodies and to $8.5^\circ$ for NACA0012.
  \item Adding $a_5$ extends this for the thin bodies, by up to $3.5^\circ$ (NACA0006, to $9^\circ$).
  \end{itemize}
\item \textbf{The range shrinks as the body thins and, for the zero-thickness plate, as Re rises.} The
  two-term radius estimate $\sqrt{|a_3/a_5|}$ is about $33^\circ$, $16^\circ$, $12^\circ$ and $10.5^\circ$
  at Re 500 (NACA0012 to zero-thickness plate). For the zero-thickness plate it falls from $18^\circ$ at
  Re $200$ to $6$--$7.5^\circ$ at Re $900$, roughly as $\mathrm{Re}^{-0.63}$.
\item \textbf{The data fix $a_1$, $a_3$, $a_5$ for every body, and $a_7$ for NACA0012 only.} The
  limit is the angular range and the content beyond the fit order, not noise (residual
  $\sim 10^{-7}$).
\end{enumerate}
\end{keybox}

\begin{warnbox}{Correction, 20 October 2026: findings 5 and 6 are superseded (Section~\ref{sec:tight})}
Every steady case in this report ran with the production solver tolerances (p $10^{-7}$, U
$10^{-8}$). Those runs stall $10^{-7}$--$2\times10^{-5}$ in $\CL$ short of the fixed point, by an error
that is smooth in $\alpha$. The fits absorb it, and the window test cannot see it. Re-converged data for
the zero-thickness plate at Re 500, 700 and 900 give:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item $a_3$ within $0.1\%$ at Re 500 and 700, and $0.5\%$ at Re 900: findings 1--3 stand;
\item $a_5 = -367$, $-376$, $-313$: biased by $3\%$ to over $50\%$ in the production data, and not
  growing with Re;
\item radius about $10.7^\circ$, $9.5^\circ$, $9.4^\circ$, not $6$--$7.5^\circ$ at Re 900, and no
  $\mathrm{Re}^{-0.63}$ law over Re 500--900.
\end{itemize}
NACA0012, NACA0006 and the thick plate have not been re-converged; their $a_5$ and $a_7$ are unverified.
\end{warnbox}

\begin{warnbox}{Withdrawn: the zero-thickness plate's $\theta^{3.4}$}
The first zero-thickness plate sweep, on an H-type grid, gave a deficit growing as $\theta^{3.4}$ and
no definable $a_3$. That was a mesh artefact.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item The H-grid's wedges at the tips reach $73$--$76^\circ$ non-orthogonality.
\item On an orthogonal elliptic-coordinate grid (maximum $4.5^\circ$), the same plate is cleanly cubic.
\end{itemize}
\end{warnbox}

\noindent\textbf{Open questions.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item What singularity of $\CL(\alpha)$ in the complex plane limits the cubic range. Candidates are
  discussed in Section~\ref{sec:sing}; the shedding Hopf is excluded.
\item Whether the shrinking range is tied to leading-edge bubble formation.
\item $a_5$ for the zero-thickness plate at Re $800$--$900$, which the $0$--$6^\circ$ data cannot fix.
\end{itemize}

\section{Setup}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Solver.} OpenFOAM (Foundation) \texttt{foamRun}, incompressible, steady SIMPLE.
  \begin{itemize}
  \item Same schemes as the transient campaign; 6000--12000 iterations.
  \item Convergence is judged on the force trace: the last-$10\%$ range of $\CL$ is at most a few
    $\times 10^{-6}$ for every case used.
  \item Warm starts from a converged neighbouring angle give identical $\CL$ and $\CD$ (checked at
    $0.5^\circ$).
  \end{itemize}
\item \textbf{Bodies and meshes.} One mesh per body and Re; the first cell is
  $h_1 = 0.05/\sqrt{\mathrm{Re}}$.
  \begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{NACA0012, NACA0006:} an O-ring, cut at $0.98c$ (blunt trailing edge).
  \item \emph{Plate, $t/c=0.023$:} a stadium section with semicircular edges of radius $0.0115c$, on a
    two-ring O-grid.
  \item \emph{Zero-thickness plate:} confocal elliptic coordinates,
    $z = c/2 + (c/2)\cosh(\mu + i\nu)$, so the plate is $\mu=0$, with $n_\nu = 800\sqrt{\mathrm{Re}/500}$.
    The plate is an internal surface, split into two walls by \texttt{createBaffles}.
  \end{itemize}
\item \textbf{Angles.}
  \begin{itemize}
  \item $0$--$6^\circ$ every $0.25^\circ$ at Re 500. The airfoils add integer angles to $20^\circ$, the
    plate $0.25^\circ$ steps to $12^\circ$.
  \item $0.5^\circ$ steps in the Re sweeps. At Re 500 the $0.5^\circ$ subset reproduces $a_3$ to
    $0.05\%$.
  \end{itemize}
\item \textbf{Machines.} OpenFOAM-dev (main machine), 13 and v14 (remote machines). Versions agree to
  about $4\times 10^{-7}$ in $\CL$; identical to six digits on the zero-thickness plate.
\end{itemize}

\section{Is the lift cubic at the origin? The deficit test}

For $\CL = a_1\theta + a_3\theta^3 + \dots$ the deficit $d = a_1\theta - \CL \to -a_3\theta^3$, so
$d/\theta^3$ tends to a constant and the local exponent $\mathrm{d}\ln d/\mathrm{d}\ln\theta$ tends to $3$.
Here $a_1$ is the Richardson limit of $\CL/\theta$ from $0.25^\circ$ and $0.5^\circ$. A non-analytic term
such as $\theta^{3+\epsilon}$ or $\theta^3\ln\theta$ would fail this.

\section{The zero-thickness plate: mesh}\label{sec:mesh}

\begin{figure}[htbp]\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{mesh_comparison.png}
\caption{Top: the H-type grid (T3), the topology of the original \texttt{.geo}. Bottom: the elliptic
grid (E800). Columns: near field; the $0.04c$ around the leading-edge tip; cell non-orthogonality.}
\end{figure}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{H-type grid.} The wedges upstream and downstream of the plate map a short vertical block
  edge onto a $45^\circ$ far-field arc.
  \begin{itemize}
  \item Non-orthogonality reaches $54^\circ$ within $0.02c$ of a tip, and $73.6^\circ$ (T3) or $76.2^\circ$ (T4)
    overall.
  \item Moving the far-field centre or making the far-field spacing uniform does not help.
  \item Tip refinement at $6^\circ$ did not converge monotonically in $\CL$.
  \end{itemize}
\item \textbf{Elliptic grid.}
  \begin{itemize}
  \item Orthogonal by construction: maximum non-orthogonality $4.5^\circ$, mostly from the span
    direction.
  \item Clusters at the tips by itself: tip cell $1.5\times10^{-5}c$ at Re 500.
  \item Only the four cells touching the tips are imperfect: each has a $180^\circ$ corner, where the map
    doubles angles.
  \end{itemize}
\item \textbf{Mesh sensitivity of $a_3$.} 1--$6^\circ$ were rerun on E560 (coarser in $\nu$).
  \begin{itemize}
  \item $\Delta\CL(\mathrm{E800}-\mathrm{E560}) = +0.0026\,\theta - 0.083\,\theta^3$, residual
    $8\times10^{-7}$: mostly a slope change.
  \item $a_3$ changes from $-12.71$ to $-12.79$ ($0.65\%$).
  \item Extrapolated, $a_3 \approx -12.8$ to $-13.0$: about $1\%$ mesh uncertainty.
  \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\section{Resolution: what the data can fix}

\textbf{Noise floor.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item Converged $\CL$ is repeatable to about $10^{-8}$.
\item The fit residual rms is $6\times10^{-8}$--$3\times10^{-7}$ to $6^\circ$, the effective noise.
\item Cross-version differences are $4\times10^{-7}$.
\item Mesh error is about $10^{-4}$ but smooth in $\alpha$, and mostly a slope change.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Test.} For each coefficient, compare its spread between windows $4$--$6^\circ$ with its
least-squares standard error. A ratio near $1$ means the coefficient is limited by noise and resolved.
A ratio much larger than $1$ means content beyond the fit order leaks into it.

\item \textbf{Resolved:} $a_1$, $a_3$ and $a_5$ for every body, and $a_7 = +3500\pm200$ for NACA0012.
\item \textbf{Not resolved:} $a_7$ for the three thinner bodies, and $a_9$ for any body.

  radius.
\item \textbf{The limit is range and truncation, not noise.} The data end at $6^\circ$ (zero-thickness
  plate), $12^\circ$ (plate) and $20^\circ$ (airfoils). A proper ratio test or Padé analysis needs at
  least four reliable coefficients. These data give two or three.
\end{itemize}

\section{Radius of convergence and the singularity question}\label{sec:sing}

The range of any truncation is bounded by the radius of convergence $R$ of the series: the distance
to the nearest singularity of $\CL(\alpha)$ in the complex plane. The two-term estimate
$\sqrt{|a_3/a_5|}$ is crude, but it orders the bodies consistently with the extrapolation ranges:
about $33^\circ$ (NACA0012), $16^\circ$ (NACA0006), $12^\circ$ (plate) and $10.5^\circ$
(zero-thickness plate).

$a_3$ & $-14.63$ & $-14.61$ & $-13.85$ & $-12.79$ & $-11.67$ & $-10.52$ & $-9.38$ & $-8.30$\\

At Re $800$ and $900$, $a_5$ depends on the fit window (the two entries are windows $0$--$5^\circ$ and

$6^\circ$ window reaches the radius itself. $a_3$ is unaffected, since its windows end at $4^\circ$. Re $800$
and $900$ at $4$--$6^\circ$ were run to $12000$ iterations, which moved $a_3$ by $0.2$--$0.3\,\%$.

\emph{[Superseded, Section~\ref{sec:tight}: the $a_5$ and radius rows above are production data.
Re-converged, the radius is about $10.7^\circ$, $9.5^\circ$ and $9.4^\circ$ at Re 500, 700 and 900.]}

The zero-thickness plate's range shrinks roughly as $\mathrm{Re}^{-0.63}$, as if a singularity moved
toward $\alpha=0$ as Re rises. The thick plate's leading-edge bubble moves the same way: formation at
$7.9^\circ$ at Re 500, $5.75^\circ$ at Re 900.

\textbf{Candidates for the singularity:}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Hopf bifurcation (shedding onset): excluded.} The steady branch continues analytically
  through it as an unstable solution, and SIMPLE computes it beyond onset (NACA0012:
  $\alpha_c=11.46^\circ$ at Re 500).
\item \textbf{Fold (saddle-node) of the steady branch: a genuine singularity.} It gives
  $\CL\sim\sqrt{\alpha_s-\alpha}$ on the real axis, with coefficients of one sign; $a_3$ and $a_5$ do share
  a sign. But NACA0006 has converged steady solutions to $20^\circ$, beyond its estimate of about
  $16^\circ$, so its nearest singularity is probably complex.
\item \textbf{Leading-edge bubble formation: not a singularity in itself.} The steady solution is
  analytic in $\alpha$ wherever the linearised problem is invertible. A sharp but smooth transition does,
  however, come with complex singularities near the real axis. A complex pair approaching the axis near
  the bubble angle would explain both the $8$--$10^\circ$ truncation failures and the shrinking $R$.
  \emph{This is a hypothesis.}
\end{itemize}

\section{Correction: the solver-tolerance bias}\label{sec:tight}

\textbf{What happened.} The production settings were p \texttt{tolerance 1e-7}, U \texttt{tolerance 1e-8},
both with \texttt{relTol 0.01}. A linear solve stops at whichever comes first, $r<$ tolerance or
$r<$ relTol$\cdot r_0$. Once $r_0$ falls below tolerance/relTol $=10^{-5}$, the absolute tolerance is
the one that binds. U solves then do $0$ iterations, p solves $0$--$2$, and SIMPLE, contracting the error
by only $\rho\approx0.998$ per iteration, stalls about step$/(1-\rho)$ from the fixed point: $10^{-7}$ to
$2\times10^{-5}$ in $\CL$, growing with $\alpha$. The ``repeatable to $10^{-8}$'' of
Section~5 is the repeatability of that stall.

\textbf{The fix.} Each case was continued with \texttt{fvSolution.tight} (tolerance $10^{-12}$, same
relTol) for $3000$ or more iterations. An angle counts as converged when its last $500$ iterations
moved $\CL$ by at most $3\times10^{-9}$, and its extrapolated remainder is also within $3\times10^{-9}$.

\textbf{Why the window test missed it.} With every angle run the same way, the error is smooth in
$\alpha$. Every window then shifts together and spread/se stays small: Re 700's production
$a_5=-405\pm1$ and $-409\pm1$ was stable and $8\%$ wrong. When the angles are run two ways (the $0.25^\circ$
quarter angles against the $0.5^\circ$ grid), neighbours stall on opposite sides and the mixed data fit
as noise (rms $4\times10^{-7}$). Only converged data can expose the bias.

At Re 900, P1--P9 is not enough on $0$--$4^\circ$ (its rms rises from $2\times10^{-9}$ to $10^{-8}$);

\textbf{Consequences.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item $a_3$ and its near-linear fall with Re (about $1.1$ per $100$) stand.
\item $|a_5|$ does not grow with Re over $500$--$900$. The $\mathrm{Re}^{-0.63}$ law rests on production
  $a_5$ and is withdrawn for that range. Re $200$--$400$, $600$ and $800$ have not been re-converged.
\item The two ratio estimates of the radius agree at each Re, and the radius shrinks only from about
  $10.7^\circ$ to $9.4^\circ$. It no longer tracks the thick plate's bubble angle.
\item \emph{Hypothesis:} at Re 900 every coefficient from $a_3$ to $a_{11}$ is negative, which points to
  a nearest singularity on the real axis near $9$--$11^\circ$, such as a fold.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Still open.} Twelve angles above about $3^\circ$ need more tight iterations; finishing them
extends the windows to $4.5$--$5^\circ$, which $a_7$ needs. The other three bodies need the same
re-convergence. The $a_5$ mesh sensitivity (E560 against E800) is unmeasured.

\section{What is established, what is not, and next steps}

\textbf{Established:}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item The steady lift of all four bodies is analytic at the origin, with a finite, nonzero, robust
  $a_3$. There is no non-analytic term.
\item $a_5$ is measured for all four bodies, and $a_7$ for NACA0012. \emph{[Section~\ref{sec:tight}:
  production $a_5$ is biased; re-converged, the zero-thickness plate's $a_5$ is resolved at Re
  500--900.]}
\item The cubic range and the two-term radius shrink with thinness, and, for the zero-thickness plate,
  with Re. \emph{[Section~\ref{sec:tight}: only from about $10.7^\circ$ to $9.4^\circ$ over Re
  500--900.]}
\item $a_3(\mathrm{Re})$ is monotonic for the airfoils. It has a maximum for both plates: near Re
  $400$--$450$ for the thick plate, and at Re $\le300$ for the zero-thickness plate.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Not established:}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item The location and type of the singularity limiting $R$.
\item Any link to bubble formation.
\item $a_7$ and beyond for the thin bodies.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Next:}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item Padé approximants in $\theta^2$, to locate complex singularities from the coefficients available.
\item Extend the zero-thickness plate to $12^\circ$ at Re 500, to fix $a_5$ and $a_7$.
\item Extend the zero-thickness plate beyond $6^\circ$ (or refine to $0.25^\circ$) at Re $800$--$900$, to
  resolve $a_5$ where the radius is smallest.
\end{enumerate}
